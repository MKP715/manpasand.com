Photography

How to prepare your home for real estate photos

Most of what makes listing photos work happens before the photographer arrives. This guide covers how to prepare your home for real estate photos room by room, outside and for privacy, plus when the light is best and what we need from you on shoot day.

GuideUpdated Nov 3, 202611 min read

Key takeaways

  1. A wide lens sees every counter, corner and fridge door at once, so clear flat surfaces down to one or two things per room.
  2. Turn on every light, match the color of your bulbs, and turn off ceiling fans and screens before the shoot starts.
  3. Put away anything that names you or shows how you live: mail, family photos, diplomas, calendars, prescriptions and valuables. Listing photos stay online long after the listing ends.
  4. MetroTex’s MLS compliance guidance says photos with people or license plates aren’t allowed, so plan for family, pets and cars to be elsewhere.
  5. Tell your photographer which way the front of the house faces; the time of day decides whether it’s in sun or shadow.

Room-by-room photo prep checklist

Buyers study listing photos. In the National Association of Realtors’ 2026 Generational Trends report, 81% of buyers who used the internet in their search rated photos “very useful,” more than any other website feature. And a wide lens sees more than you do: both nightstands, the corners and the floor under the window in one frame, and buyers can zoom in. Anything on a flat surface will be in the picture.

The goal isn’t an empty house, but one where everything in the frame is there on purpose. What comes off the counters can go in a closed cabinet, a bin in the garage or a car trunk.

The phone-camera test

Before the photographer arrives, stand in each doorway and take a wide photo with your phone. A photo shows the clutter your eye has stopped noticing: the charger on the counter, the towel on the oven door, the shoes by the bed.

Outside: the first photo buyers see

The front of the house is usually the first photo in the listing. Most of the prep is yard work. Mow and edge a day or two before, so the cut looks fresh but the clippings have settled. If your sprinklers run on a timer, skip the cycle the morning of the shoot so the driveway and walk are dry.

Late summer is hard on lawns around Rowlett. At DFW Airport, July and August are the two driest months of the year, with normal highs near 96°F and about 2 inches of rain each, according to NOAA’s 1991–2020 normals. If the grass is browning, water it in the weeks before the shoot, on the days your city’s watering schedule allows. Don’t plan on editing to fix it: the North Texas MLS (NTREIS) bars digital changes that misrepresent a property’s condition, and MetroTex Association of Realtors warns that unrealistically green grass or landscaping that doesn’t exist can do exactly that.

  • Trash and recycling carts in the garage or out of sight, not at the curb
  • Hoses coiled out of view; yard tools, toys and bikes put away
  • Pet waste picked up and dog toys collected from the yard
  • Garage doors closed and seasonal decorations taken down, since a wreath dates the photos for as long as the listing runs
  • Porch and walk swept, cobwebs knocked down, doormat clean or gone
  • Patio cushions out and the grill cover off, or the grill wheeled out of view
  • Pool skimmed, with the automatic cleaner, hoses and cover put away

Lights, blinds and timing

Turn on every light, including lamps, under-cabinet lights and the range hood, even on a sunny day. Interior lights fill the shadows daylight leaves. Replace burned-out bulbs the week before, and match them to the bulbs already in the room. A lamp with a warm bulb next to a ceiling fixture with a cool one shows both colors in the photo, and editing only partly evens them out. The FTC’s Lighting Facts label on bulb packages shows the bulb’s light appearance on a warm (more yellow) to cool (more blue) scale, with a number in kelvins, so buy the same number you already have.

Turn off ceiling fans, whose blades blur, and TVs, monitors and smart displays. Open blinds and curtains the same amount in every room, and clean the windows that face a view. Rowlett sits on Lake Ray Hubbard, and if your windows look out over it, the view may be the best thing in the listing. We balance the exposure so it shows through the glass instead of as a white rectangle.

Time of day matters most for the front of the house. From November through January, the sun rises in the southeast and sets in the southwest, so it stays on the south side of a house all day and a north-facing front sits in shade. From May through July, it rises in the northeast and sets in the northwest. Tell us which way your front door faces and we’ll suggest a morning or afternoon time so the sun is on the front, not behind the house. Weather is the other variable: May is the wettest month at DFW Airport on average, so for a spring shoot, keep a backup day in mind and ask your agent how much room the listing date has.

What to put away for privacy and security

Listing photos are public, and they travel. Under NTREIS rules, an agent who puts photos in the MLS gives the MLS and its other brokers and agents the right to reproduce and display them, so your kitchen can show up on sites your agent doesn’t run. The same rules keep your name off the public listing: Rule 16.03 bars MLS members from showing customers a seller’s or occupant’s name, phone number or email. Don’t let a photo put it back.

The National Association of Realtors’ Safe Listing Form for sellers is a good starting list: valuables, from mail on the counter to jewelry, cash, artwork, laptops, phones and gaming systems; personal items like diplomas, awards, family photos, wall calendars and reminder boards; all prescription medications; and firearms. The camera adds a few more:

  • Anything with your name, your kids’ names or their school: mail, packages, monogrammed towels, name letters, uniforms, team photos
  • Calendars, whiteboards and smart displays that show when the house will be empty
  • Prescription bottles, labels and all: in a drawer or with you
  • Bills, bank statements, tax forms, checkbooks and medical paperwork
  • The Wi‑Fi password on a card or the router’s label (turn the sticker to the wall); if you still use that default, the FTC recommends changing it
  • Spare keys on hooks and any door or garage codes written down
  • Safes, gun cabinets and anything that shows how your home is secured: tell us where they are, and we’ll keep them out of the frame where we can

Listing photos outlive the listing

NTREIS rules bar its members from displaying a listing’s photos, except the main photo, once the home has been sold or off the market for more than 12 months. Other sites set their own rules. Zillow’s help pages say that to hide or remove photos from a home’s page, you claim the home as its owner, and after closing the owner is the buyer. Prepare as if your interior photos will be public for years.

People, pets and cars

This part is MLS compliance, not just looks. MetroTex Association of Realtors, a Dallas-area Realtor association, says listing photos that include people (especially children) or license plates aren’t allowed, citing NTREIS Rule 7.29. Your house number is a different case: the address is in the listing itself, so covering the number on the front doesn’t make it private.

Move every car off the driveway and away from the curb in front of the house, including the one you arrive in. If the garage will be photographed, the cars come out of it too, so park down the street.

Pets are easiest away from home for the length of the shoot: a walk, a neighbor’s yard, daycare. If that won’t work, crate them in a room we’ve already photographed and move the crate as we go. Then put away their bowls, beds, toys, leashes and litter boxes.

A timeline for preparing your home for real estate photos

Spread the work over the week before. This schedule front-loads the slow jobs and leaves shoot day for a quick reset.

  1. A week before: Fix what shows: burned-out bulbs, a loose cabinet door, scuffed paint by light switches. Decide where the clutter will go.
  2. Two or three days before: Clean windows and mirrors and take down seasonal decorations.
  3. The day before: Mow and edge, finish the laundry, clean the kitchen and bathrooms, and put away mail, medications, paperwork and photos.
  4. Shoot day: Make the beds, clear the counters, empty the trash, skip the sprinklers and set out fresh towels.
  5. An hour before: Lights on, fans and screens off, blinds even, toilet lids down, cars and trash carts moved. Do the phone-camera test.
  6. When we arrive: Walk us through and point out what to show and what to skip, then head out with the kids and pets, or stay a room ahead of us.
  7. After we leave: Put your house back. Edited photos arrive on the delivery date in your quote.

What we need from you

When you ask for a quote, send the address, the square footage, the number of bedrooms and baths, and the date the listing should go live. We use that to set the shot list, the price and the delivery date, in writing, before the shoot. Then, before shoot day, tell us:

  • Access: who will let us in, and any gate, HOA, garage or pool-gate codes we’ll need
  • Power: electricity on and a working bulb in every fixture; this matters most in a vacant house
  • Features to show: updates buyers should notice, like a remodeled kitchen, new flooring, a lake view or a covered patio
  • Anything off-limits: rooms, items or angles you don’t want photographed
  • Your agent’s needs: any specific shots the listing calls for
  • Which way the front faces, if you know, so we can time the exterior

Questions

Q.01Should lights be on or off for real estate photos?

On, all of them, lamps and under-cabinet lights included, even when the sun is out; they fill the shadows daylight leaves. Turn off ceiling fans and TVs, and swap burned-out bulbs for ones that match the color of the rest of the room.

Q.02What should I remove before listing photos?

Anything personal or valuable, and most of what sits on flat surfaces: mail, family photos, diplomas, calendars, prescriptions, jewelry, laptops, toiletries, small appliances and everything on the fridge. Leave the furniture and a few simple decorative pieces. The table above goes room by room.

Q.03Should I be home during the real estate photo shoot?

You don’t have to be, as long as someone can let us in. If you stay, plan to keep a room ahead of us or step outside, because MLS compliance guidance says listing photos can’t include people. Kids and pets are easier away from home until we’re done.

Q.04How long does a real estate photo shoot take?

It depends on the size of the house and the shot list. We estimate the time when we book, so you know how long the house has to stay photo-ready. Keep it that way from our arrival until we leave, because a towel left on a counter means reshooting that room.

Q.05Can you edit out things I forget to put away?

Put it away instead. Moving a bottle takes a minute; editing it out of every angle that shows it takes longer and rarely looks as clean. We won’t edit out anything that’s part of the house, like cracks, stains or a brown lawn, because NTREIS Rule 7.30 bars digital changes that misrepresent a property’s condition.
