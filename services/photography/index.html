Real estate photos, headshots and portraits

§ 01  Scope

What’s included

Ground-level listing photos, headshots, event coverage, family portraits and product shots, with a fixed price and written usage rights.

  • Listing photos of interiors and exteriors, all taken from the ground
  • MLS-ready images: no logos, borders, text or people in the frame
  • Color, exposure and vertical lines corrected; no defects edited out
  • Individual and team headshots with matching background and light
  • Corporate event coverage planned from your run-of-show
  • Family portraits timed around light, weather and nap schedules
  • Product photos sized for your website or online store
  • A written license spelling out how you may use the photos
  • Model releases for identifiable people; a guardian signs for kids
  • Fixed-price quote before the shoot, any sales tax on its own line

Who it’s for

  • Listing agents and home sellers in Rowlett, Garland and Rockwall
  • Small businesses that need team headshots or product photos
  • Companies hosting meetings, launches, award nights or parties
  • Families who want portraits at home or at a favorite outdoor spot

Real estate photography in Rowlett, TX, shot to MLS rules

If your listing goes into NTREIS, the North Texas MLS, its rules decide what the photos may show. Rule 7.30 allows only a true, current photograph of the property, without decorative borders, embellishments or digital changes that would misrepresent its condition or appearance, and without the agent’s name, phone number, email or web address. MetroTex Association of Realtors’ compliance notes add that photos showing people (especially children), license plates or QR codes aren’t allowed.

So we shoot listings from the ground, inside and out, with nobody in the frame: cars off the driveway, family and pets out of the room, and our name nowhere on the images. Editing fixes what the camera gets wrong, not what the house gets wrong. We correct color and exposure, straighten vertical lines and balance bright windows so the view shows instead of a white rectangle, which matters if your Rowlett house looks out over Lake Ray Hubbard. We don’t paint out cracks or stains, green up a brown lawn or remove anything a buyer will see at the showing. If your agent adds virtual staging later, MetroTex’s guidance is that staging can show how an empty room might look furnished, but digital renovations and made-up landscaping can misrepresent the home.

Photos do much of the first showing. In NAR’s 2026 Home Buyers and Sellers Generational Trends report, 81% of buyers who used the internet rated photos “very useful,” more than any other website feature. Most of what makes them work happens before we arrive, and our listing photo prep guide walks through it room by room, privacy steps included. Rowlett is our home base. A listing in Garland is about 13 minutes / 7 miles away without traffic, and one in Rockwall about 20 minutes / 10 miles. Short drives make it easier to shoot the front of the house when the sun is on it.

Headshots and corporate events: what to expect

A headshot session is short. We set up a plain background and soft, even light, take enough frames for you to pick an expression you like, and look through them with you before we pack up. For a team, we set up once, in a conference room or an empty office, and keep the background, light and framing the same for everyone, so the faces on your website match.

Event coverage starts with your run-of-show. We turn it into a shot list: speakers, awards, sponsor banners, the group photo and the people who can’t be missed. Ballrooms and offices are often dimmer than they look, so we ask about the room and whether flash is OK during talks. Tell attendees that photos will be taken, with a line on the registration page and a sign at the door, and give anyone who opts out an easy marker, such as a different-colored lanyard, so we can leave them out of the published set.

Family portraits and product photos

Family sessions work best around short attention spans: a place the kids know, a time before naps, and soft light rather than midday sun. North Texas summers narrow the outdoor options. NOAA’s 1991–2020 normals put the average high at DFW Airport near 96°F in July and August, so keep summer sessions short and in the shade, or move them indoors. If you want early-morning or evening light, ask when you book and we’ll tell you what we can schedule. May is DFW’s wettest month on average, so a spring date should come with a rain date.

If you have a spot in mind, we check its rules before we book. Some places regulate paid portrait sessions: Fort Worth requires a permit for commercial and portrait photography in its parks, and the Dallas Arboretum charges an extra fee for commercial sessions and allows professional photography with more than a handheld camera only from 3 to 5 PM.

Product photos need consistency more than drama: the same background, angle and light, so a page of products reads as a set. Tell us where the pictures will live. Shopify says square images at 2048 × 2048 px usually display best, and Etsy recommends listing photos at least 2,000 pixels wide and tall, so we leave room to crop for each. Headed for a new website? We can size and place them as part of a web development project.

Files, ownership and permission

You get edited JPEG files sized for how you’ll use them: full resolution for print, smaller copies for the web, email and the MLS, and square crops of headshots for profile pictures. The delivery date is set in the quote, and we don’t put watermarks on the files you pay for.

Under US copyright law, copyright starts with the photographer, and transferring it takes a signed writing. We keep the copyright and give you a written license that fits the job: MLS and marketing use for a listing, website and advertising use for business photos, personal use for family portraits. NTREIS advises agents to get a written agreement from their photographer that either assigns the copyright or lets the agent grant NTREIS a license to publish the photos; ours includes the second. If you need to own the copyright outright, say so before we quote. One catch for sellers: if a home is relisted with a different brokerage, NTREIS requires written consent from the former listing broker before the old photos can be reused in the MLS.

Anyone identifiable in photos your business will use for marketing signs a model release, and a parent or guardian signs for children. Texas law recognizes a claim when someone’s likeness is used for its value without consent, and a signed release answers that question before the photos go public. We also ask before showing any client’s photos in our own portfolio, and our reviews page quotes clients word for word. When we rebuilt the Mahnken Painting & More website, house numbers and family photos in the project pictures were blurred and location data was stripped before anything went live.

All FAQs
Q.01How much does real estate photography cost?

It depends on the size of the house, how many edited photos the listing needs, the drive and the deadline. We don’t publish a price list. We look at the listing and send a fixed price before the shoot, with Texas sales tax, if it applies, on its own line. Ask for a quote with the address, square footage and target listing date.

Q.02How soon will I get my real estate photos back?

We set the delivery date in the quote, before the shoot, so you can plan the day the listing goes live. If you’re working to a tight launch date, tell us when you first get in touch and we’ll say plainly whether we can meet it.

Q.03How many photos does a listing need?

Enough to walk a buyer through the house without gaps: the front, every main living space, the kitchen from more than one angle, each bedroom and bathroom, the backyard and anything special, like a lake view or a new patio. Skip closets and hallways that show nothing new. We agree on the number in the quote.

Q.04Are your exterior photos all taken from the ground?

Yes. Every photo we take, inside and out, is taken from the ground: from the street, the driveway, the yard and the back patio. That’s where buyers stand at a showing, too.

Q.05What should I wear for a professional headshot?

Wear what you’d wear to meet a client, in solid, mid-tone colors. Fine stripes and small checks can shimmer on screens, and big logos pull attention from your face. Bring a second top, and if you wear glasses, wear your everyday pair. For a team, agree on a dress code first: one patterned shirt in a row of solids is the photo everyone notices.

Q.06How should I prepare my house for listing photos?

Clear counters and tabletops, turn on every light and put away personal items: mail, family photos, medicine and anything with your name on it. Move cars off the driveway and plan for people and pets to be out of the shot. Our room-by-room prep guide has the full checklist.
