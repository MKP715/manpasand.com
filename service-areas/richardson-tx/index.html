From Collins Radio to the IQ: who works in Richardson now

Richardson became a technology town in the 1950s. Central Expressway opened through the city in 1954, Collins Radio bought land for a Richardson office in 1955 and opened it in 1958, and that same year Texas Instruments opened a plant just south of the city line. In 1969, TI executives donated a research center, the Southwest Center for Advanced Studies, to the state, and it became the University of Texas at Dallas. The city still calls the area along US 75 the Telecom Corridor, and it says more than 88,000 people work in Richardson each day. About two-thirds of the city is in Dallas County; the rest is in Collin.

The big names are easy to spot: State Farm and Raytheon at CityLine, the 186-acre development at US 75 and the Bush Turnpike, and UT Dallas, with 30,139 students in fall 2025. The city’s 1,200-acre Innovation Quarter, the IQ, counts more than 1,000 businesses on its own. Most Richardson businesses are small. Census Bureau ZIP Code Business Patterns for 2023 count about 4,480 business locations with paid employees in ZIP codes 75080, 75081 and 75082, and 82 percent have fewer than 20 employees. Of those locations, 735 are professional, scientific and technical firms, the Census category for law, accounting, engineering, design, computer and consulting offices.

That’s the scale we work at. A six-person engineering office needs the same basics as a big one, without an IT department to own them: a firewall someone manages, Wi‑Fi that keeps guests off the staff network, PCs and printers set up the same way, and a database somebody understands. We set those up and give you a fixed-price quote before any work starts.

Meeting rooms when half the meeting isn’t in the room

Richardson has four DART light-rail stations: Spring Valley, Arapaho Center, Galatyn Park and CityLine/Bush. Since October 25, 2025, the Silver Line has also stopped at UT Dallas and CityLine/Bush on its 26-mile run between DFW Airport and Plano. Census survey estimates for 2020–2024 show about a quarter of Richardson’s working residents, roughly 16,000 people, work from home. Between rail commuters and home workers, plenty of meetings here have some people at the table and some on a screen.

For a small room, the choice usually comes down to two setups. With bring-your-own-laptop, a USB camera bar and speaker plug into whichever laptop is running the meeting. It costs less and works with Teams, Zoom or anything else, but it depends on that laptop and that cable. A dedicated room system starts the meeting with one tap. For Teams, Microsoft’s Teams Rooms Basic license comes at no extra cost with Microsoft-approved Teams Rooms hardware, for up to 25 rooms; dual screens, remote device management and some other features need the paid Pro license. Either way, the camera belongs at the screen, the microphones need to reach the far chair, and the room should be on a wired connection, not the guest Wi‑Fi.

Our conference room video setup checklist walks through those decisions, or we can set up the room for you.

On-site IT support in Richardson, and what to check first

Richardson is about 20 minutes and 12 miles from Rowlett without traffic, most of it on the Bush Turnpike, a toll road. Network installs, office moves, meeting rooms and home-office visits happen on-site, Mon–Fri, 9 AM – 5 PM. Database work and web projects can be done remotely. We respond within one business day and acknowledge critical support requests within two hours during business hours.

A few local details save time. The city says most residents can get fiber or cable internet (its guide lists AT&T and Spectrum), with DSL in some areas. Before you sign a lease, look the suite up on the FCC National Broadband Map, which lists the providers, service types and speeds reported for each address. Ask the landlord where the provider’s line enters the building and whether you can run network cable to your suite. You can choose your electricity company, but Oncor handles power outages; the city lists Oncor’s outage line as 1-888-313-4747. A battery backup for the modem, firewall and switch keeps a short flicker from dropping the whole office offline.

For the home offices behind those video calls, the work laptop often shares the house network with TVs, game consoles and smart speakers. We can put those devices on their own network and run a cable to the desk where Wi‑Fi is weak. Our home network and Wi‑Fi security page covers the details.

Q.01Do you offer on-site IT support in Richardson?

Q.02Can you set up a small conference room for Teams or Zoom?

Q.03Do you offer remote IT and database support?

Network gear, PC hardware and meeting rooms are hands-on jobs, so in Richardson we come to you. Q.04What should a small office network include?

At minimum: a firewall you control (not only the internet provider’s box), a switch with spare ports, business Wi‑Fi with a separate guest network, a separate network for printers, TVs and other smart devices, and battery backup for the modem, firewall and switch. Keep the admin passwords and settings written down somewhere other than the network itself. In a shared office building, find out what the landlord controls before changing anything.
