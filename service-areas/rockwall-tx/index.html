On-site IT support in Rockwall, from across the lake

Rockwall is the seat of Rockwall County, which the Texas State Historical Association calls the smallest county in Texas. The city sits on the east shore of Lake Ray Hubbard, and we’re across the water in Rowlett. Two highways cross the water between us: the SH 66 bridge, which links the two cities directly, and I‑30, which runs over the lake on a 2.9-mile causeway farther south. Center to center, the drive is about 20 minutes / 10 miles without traffic, and the quickest route uses SH 66.

I‑30 is the crossing to watch. TxDOT is widening it across the lake and adding frontage-road bridges, with the project expected to finish by early 2028. Until then, the City of Rockwall notes, an incident on the lake bridge can cause bottlenecks and road closures. When a visit depends on the interstate, we check TxDOT’s closure notices before we set a time.

Most of this work happens on-site: office networks and Wi‑Fi, network cabling for computers, phones and Wi‑Fi, PCs, printers and point-of-sale networks, meeting-room video setups and photography. Database and web projects can be done remotely, so the lake doesn’t matter for those. Either way, the first consultation is free, and you get a detailed scope and a fixed-price quote before any work begins.

A fast-growing city, and networks set up in a hurry

Census Bureau estimates put Rockwall at 53,980 people in July 2025, up about 14% from 47,308 at the April 2020 base. Rockwall County grew faster, by about 30%, from 107,884 to 140,738. Growth like that means new offices, storefronts and restaurants, and a new location’s network often goes in during the rush to open: the internet provider’s router on its default settings, one Wi‑Fi name shared by staff, customers and the card terminal, and nobody quite sure who has the admin password.

If you run a restaurant or shop near The Harbor, the lakefront dining and entertainment district just south of I‑30, guest Wi‑Fi is probably part of the service. The FTC’s small-business cybersecurity guides are plain about it: Wi‑Fi for guests and customers should be separate from, and not connected to, your business network. They also say to change the router’s default name and password, turn off remote management, use WPA2 or WPA3 encryption, and require multi-factor authentication for the parts of your network that hold sensitive information.

When we secure a Rockwall office or shop, the network security work usually covers:

  • A map of what’s connected: modem, router, switches, access points, PCs, printers and the POS
  • Separate networks for guests, staff devices and payment equipment
  • New admin passwords, and remote management turned off where you don’t use it
  • WPA2 or WPA3 on every access point, with firmware brought up to date
  • Multi-factor sign-in on email and admin accounts

Power and internet: know who to call before something fails

Electric service in Rockwall isn’t one utility. Depending on the address, the lines in and around Rockwall belong to Oncor or to Farmers Electric Cooperative, a member-owned co-op based in Greenville that serves parts of 12 Texas counties, Rockwall County among them. Outages are reported to whichever utility owns your lines, so check your bill and save the right outage number before the next storm.

A blip in the middle of a lunch rush can drop the modem, the router, the card terminal and the back-office PC at once. A battery backup (UPS) under the network gear and the POS keeps them running through short outages and gives computers time to shut down cleanly. Our guide to protecting office tech from power outages covers sizing and what to plug in where.

For internet, look before you sign a lease or move into a new subdivision. The FCC’s National Broadband Map lists the providers, technology (fiber, cable, DSL, fixed wireless or satellite) and maximum advertised speeds reported for each address. It shows what’s available, not how well it performs, so we’re glad to help you compare the options or weigh a second connection as a backup if your business can’t go offline.

All FAQs
Q.01Do you offer on-site IT support in Rockwall?

Yes. We’re based in Rowlett, across the lake, and we come to Rockwall offices, shops and homes Mon–Fri, 9 AM – 5 PM. We don’t have an office in Rockwall. We respond within one business day, and critical support requests are acknowledged within two hours during business hours.

Q.02Who provides electricity in Rockwall, Oncor or Farmers Electric?

Both serve the Rockwall area, and which one you have depends on the address, so check your electric bill. Report outages to the utility that owns your lines: Oncor at (888) 313-4747 (or text OUT to 66267), or Farmers Electric Cooperative at (903) 455-1715. Both numbers appear on the Public Utility Commission of Texas’s list of utility outage contacts.

Q.03How do I secure a small business network in Rockwall?

Start with the FTC’s basics: change the router’s default name and password, turn off remote management, use WPA2 or WPA3, keep guest Wi‑Fi separate from your business network and require multi-factor authentication wherever sensitive information lives. Then set software to update automatically and back up important files. If you’d rather have it done, we’ll come out for a free consultation, check each item and quote a fixed price for whatever needs fixing.

Q.04Do you also serve Heath and Fate?

Yes. Heath borders Rockwall to the south and shares its 75032 ZIP code; it’s about 24 minutes / 13 miles from Rowlett without traffic. Fate, east of Rockwall along SH 66, is about 26 minutes / 14 miles. We also make on-site visits to Royse City.
