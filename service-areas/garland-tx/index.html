Power outages in Garland: who to call, and what to protect

Much of Garland gets its electricity from the city itself. Garland Power & Light (GP&L) is the city’s not-for-profit municipal utility. It has served Garland since 1923 and has more than 77,000 customers. Other addresses are on Oncor’s lines instead. GP&L points out that you and your neighbor may have different providers, so check your electric bill to see which one you have.

That decides who you call when the power drops. For GP&L, call 972-205-3000 or 833-403-2106, or text OUT to 972-205-4000 from the cell number on your account. GP&L recommends confirming that number before you need it. If you’re on Oncor, call 888-313-4747 or text OUT to 66267. Your internet provider can’t bring the power back, and neither can we.

Our job comes before and after the outage. Before: a battery backup (UPS) on the modem, router, network switch and POS, so a short blip doesn’t reboot the network in the middle of a sale. We also set up a server or NAS (a network storage box) to shut down cleanly during a long outage instead of crashing. A UPS only covers your side, though. If the outage also knocks out your internet provider’s equipment, the connection drops anyway. After: we check whatever didn’t come back up. Our guide to protecting office tech from outages explains how to size a UPS.

Your surge protection is worth a second look too. GP&L’s advice: not every power strip is a surge protector, and surge protectors wear out. A whole-house protector at the panel or meter only guards against surges on the electric lines. It doesn’t cover surges on phone or TV cable lines, and if you have cable internet, that coax line goes straight into your modem. Installing a whole-house unit is an electrician’s job. Plug-in protection for your computers and network gear is ours.

Shops, suppliers and small manufacturers

Garland’s economic development office counts more than 300 manufacturers in the city. Its largest employers include Kraft Heinz, Sherwin-Williams and Epiroc, along with Garland ISD and the city itself. We don’t pitch to plants that size. We work with the smaller businesses around them: the supplier with a front office and a warehouse out back, the store with two registers, the five-person office near Downtown Garland or Firewheel Town Center.

If your building is half office and half warehouse, you may already know the problems: Wi‑Fi that fades past the office door, a label printer that keeps dropping off the network, and one Wi‑Fi password that every employee, former employee and delivery driver has had for years. We map where you actually need coverage, then add access points or office network cabling for computers, phones and Wi‑Fi. Then we split the network so office PCs, card terminals and guest devices can’t reach each other.

For a store, the POS network comes first. Card terminals go on their own segment behind a locked-down firewall, and guest Wi‑Fi can’t see them. If customer data is ever exposed anyway, Texas law sets deadlines for notifying the people affected, and in larger breaches the attorney general. Our Texas data breach notification guide walks through them. For day-to-day help, see our IT help for Garland shops and our network security for small businesses.

Q.01Is there small business IT support in Garland without a contract?

Our IT help is on demand. You contact us when something needs doing, we scope it in a free consultation, and you get a fixed-price quote before any work begins. We reply within one business day and acknowledge critical support requests within two hours during business hours.

Q.02Who do I contact about a power outage in Garland?

Your electric utility, not your internet provider or us. Much of Garland is on Garland Power & Light: call 972-205-3000 or 833-403-2106, or text OUT to 972-205-4000. Some addresses are on Oncor instead (888-313-4747, or text OUT to 66267), and your bill shows which one you have. Once the power is back, we can check the network and anything that didn’t come back up on its own.

Q.03Can you set up a POS network for a Garland shop?

Yes. We set up the network your POS runs on: the internet connection, the firewall, a separate segment for card terminals, guest Wi‑Fi that can’t reach it, and battery backup for the modem and router. Your POS provider still handles the software and your card-processing account, and we build the network to their requirements.

Q.04Do you also help Garland homeowners?

Yes. About 1 in 8 Garland workers works from home, so a home network often carries work too. We set up and secure home Wi‑Fi and networks, including better coverage for the office and a separate network for TVs, speakers and thermostats, and we also take family portraits. If you’re choosing an internet provider, the FCC National Broadband Map shows which ones report service at your address.
